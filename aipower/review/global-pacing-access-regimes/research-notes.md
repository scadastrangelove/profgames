# Global pacing and access regimes — research notes

Reviewed on 14 September 2026 against the pinned v0.36 bilingual atlas pair.

## Accepted

- **Seoul, 21 May 2024.** The source establishes voluntary risk thresholds, pre-deployment assessment and an extreme commitment not to develop or deploy when mitigations cannot keep risk below threshold. It does not establish a joint training calendar or government licence.
- **UK AISI, 24 October 2024.** AISI disclosed completed pre-deployment tests; its 2025 trends report confirms checkpoint access before public release in some cases. The relationship remains collaborative rather than a general statutory veto.
- **China, 13 May 2026.** CAC reported 72 new service filings and 49 app/feature registrations for March-April, with cumulative totals of 868 and 530 through 30 April. This is implementation evidence for a public-service gate, not evidence of model-training pacing.

## Existing records reused

- China's 2023 Interim Measures establish security-assessment and algorithm-filing duties for public-facing services.
- China's 2026 agent-governance opinions extend lifecycle, permission-boundary and recall language but remain high-level guidance.
- EU AI Act enforcement can require information and model access for evaluation and can restrict public availability within its statutory scope.
- South Korea's sovereign-model and cyber-model programmes concern provenance, continuity and exit from foreign suppliers, not capability pacing.

## Comparative conclusion

Outside the United States, authority over advanced AI is visible in at least five distinct forms: voluntary development/deployment thresholds, pre-release evaluator visibility, binding market-access powers, filing gates for public services, and sovereign procurement or replacement programmes. These mechanisms support a common structural-power question but not a claim that jurisdictions have converged on one policy or that global training has measurably slowed.

## Source-quality boundary

The new records use official first-party pages; the International AI Safety Report is retained as a high-authority synthesis for the implementation caveat. Counts reported by CAC are official administrative totals, not an independent audit of the registered services.
